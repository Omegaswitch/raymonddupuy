## Q1. Date and authorship of the Rule

**Evidence (S2, ch. 1 "Origins, c.1070-1160", section "The Rule", printed pp. 24-26).**
- Gerard may have had a Rule, "it has not survived, although elements of it may have passed into the one that was drawn up by Raymond of Puy" (p. 24).
- Alexander III (1172) refers to "the regulations and statutes of Gerard and R[aymond] of good memory" (p. 24, n. 84: PTJ 2:229 no. 20).
- Lucius III (1184/5) refers to the rule "quam pie recordationis Raymundus, magister vester, de communi consilio et voluntate capituli, salubriter ordinavit" (n. 86: Pflugk-Harttung, *Acta pontificum inedita* 2:389 no. 441 = PTJ 1:361 no. 172). This is the firmest contemporary evidence that the Rule is Raymond's, made with chapter's counsel and consent.
- Riley-Smith: "It cannot be dated precisely" (p. 25). Thirteenth-century tradition: confirmed by Innocent II (1130-43), n. 95. A 1143 reference to brothers living "regulariter" (n. 96: Cart Hosp 1:123 no. 154). "a Rule was confirmed by Pope Eugenius III, who died in 1153" (n. 97, citing Acta pont. ined. 2:389 no. 441; Hiestand; Luttrell).
- Argument for a late date: the Rule mentions priests, and "only in 1154 ... the papal privilege *Christiane fidei religio* gave the Order the right to have them" (pp. 25-26). Riley-Smith rejects it: the Order had priests earlier, some priest clauses look "crudely interpolated", so "no reason why the Rule we have ... should not date from the 1130s", possibly "the first half of the 1130s" (before the Bethgibelin gift of 1136). He notes Beltjens prefers 1145-53 (n. 95).
- Composition: 19 clauses; the first 15 "seem to have comprised the Rule proper", the last four are later additions "composed at any time before the mid-1180s" (p. 25; n. 91 cites Delaville Le Roulx, "Les statuts", BEC 48 (1887); Luttrell, "The Hospitallers' Early Statutes", Revue Mabillon 75 (2003)).
- Survival: the authoritative manuscript "was apparently lost" in 1187; a form approved by the pope in 1185 survived, plus versions of 1206 and 1300, "the last becoming the version that was considered to be standard" (p. 24).
- S1 (Sinclair intro, via summary): Latin text of unknown completion date; "confirmed in the last years of the pontificate of Eugenius III (1145-53)" (cites Cartulaire no. 217); Lucius III (1181-85) confirmed it as an Augustinian rule (cites Cartulaire no. 690). Raymond's death placed 1158-60.
- S3 headnote: "Rule c.1150", "earliest extant MS c. 1253", attribution "A.D. 1120-60" (translator's/site's date, not an argument).
- S4: "written in the first half of the 12th century under the leadership of Raymond du Puy"; "solemnly approved ... from Lucius III (1184) to Boniface VIII (1300)". Devotional, not a critical study.
- S5 (via summary): first rule "compiled under Master Raymond du Puy before 1153"; *Christiane fidei religio* "in 1154" allowed admission of priests.

**Conclusion.** Authorship by Raymond (master c.1118/20-c.1158/60) rests on the Lucius III letter and Alexander III's 1172 reference. Riley-Smith: undatable precisely, possibly 1130s, certainly before the Eugenius III confirmation (d. 1153); the 19-clause text we have is known as it stood from 1185. King's "1120-60" and "confirmed 1145-53" are consistent with this, but I did not open King, so King's own wording is NOT VERIFIED.

**Correction to the brief.** *Christiane fidei religio* is dated **1154** by both Riley-Smith (p. 26) and Sarnowsky, not 1153. Search snippets (not opened) attribute it to Anastasius IV; this is NOT VERIFIED here. I did not see the text of any Eugenius III bull called "Christiane fidei religio" and I found no source that ties it to Eugenius's confirmation. Treat "Eugenius III's bull of 1153" as unsupported.

**Uncertainty.** Riley-Smith 1967 (*Knights of St John in Jerusalem and Cyprus*) not opened, so his earlier position is NOT VERIFIED. Luttrell, Beltjens, Hiestand not opened. Page numbers for S2 are the printed book pages as they appear in the PDF text.

## Q2. Latin vs Old French tradition

**Evidence.**
- Delaville Le Roulx, *Cartulaire général*, 4 vols, Paris 1894-1906 (S2 abbreviation list; S6 confirms 4 vols and 1894-1906, documents arranged chronologically). S3 states the Latin Rule is in "tome I, Paris, 1894, pp. 62-68, doc. 70". S1 and S2 do not give the number; S1 cites "Cartulaire, no. 70" for the Latin Regula (consistent). S1 further gives Cart. no. 217 (Eugenius III) and no. 690 (Lucius III), unconfirmed elsewhere. The Cartulaire scan itself NOT opened; S6 says Wikipedia links BNP scans of vols 1-4 (not opened).
- Which Cartulaire number holds the **Old French** version: NOT VERIFIED. S2 n. 90 points to Delaville Le Roulx, *Les Hospitaliers en Terre Sainte et à Chypre* (1904), pp. 43, 46, 318-19 and to Luttrell, Klement ("Le prime tre redazioni della Regola Giovannita", Studi Melitensi 4, 1996; and Vat. Lat. 4852, Studi Melitensi 3, 1995), Beltjens, *Aux origines de l'Ordre de Malte* (Brussels 1995, pp. 278-304) and Sinclair.
- Riley-Smith (p. 24-25): the Rules of 1185 and 1206 "were lost in the fall of Acre in 1291, but they had already been copied in French translation by or for William of Santo Stefano"; copies in translation made at provincial centres survive; "knowledge of the Rule as it applied from 1185 onwards".
- Sinclair (S1): the Anglo-Norman *Riwle* is a **verse** translation of the Latin Regula, surviving only in Cambridge, Corpus Christi College MS 405, pp. 255-310 (hand late 13th/early 14th c.); date of composition argued as "later rather than earlier" 12th century, "may well date from 1181-85". This is the Anglo-Norman text, not necessarily the Old French prose text King used; King's source manuscript(s) NOT VERIFIED.
- S2 n. 26 (ch. 4) refers to "Rule §§9 Ver. Lat., 13 Ver. Lat.", showing Riley-Smith treats a Latin version (Ver. Lat.) as a separate witness to the French; detail NOT VERIFIED.

**Conclusion.** Latin Regula: Cart. I no. 70 (pp. 62-68 per S3). Vernacular: several French/Anglo-Norman translations of the 1185/1206 forms; standard late form is 1300 (Boniface VIII). Old French date: not later than 13th c. copies; the Anglo-Norman verse perhaps 1181-85 (Sinclair, conjectural).

**Uncertainty.** I could not open Delaville Le Roulx vol. 1, Luttrell 2003, Klement, or King. Which numbered Cartulaire document contains the French text: unresolved.

## Q3. "Warden" / "servant of Christ's poor" formula

**Evidence.**
- Latin opening per S3: "Ego Raymundus, servus pauperum Christi et custos Hospitalis Ierosolimitani" (via summary; page also gives English "I, Raymond, servant of the poor of Christ and custodian of the Jerusalem Hospital").
- S1 cites the same Latin phrase from the Cartulaire; it does not translate *custos*.
- S4 prints an English version: "I Raymond Servant of Christ's poor and Warden of the Hospital of Jerusalem" (printed p. 8; this is King-style wording). The text says Raymond acted "with the counsel of all the Chapter, both clerical and lay brethren". It comments: "A 'servant' of the poor, the Order's Master is also their 'guardian'" (p. 10).
- Riley-Smith (S2, author's note on titles in the preliminary matter; page not recorded): officers' titles "servus, minister, prior, magister, gubernator ..." denote government of people; "procurator ... dominus, bajulus" the management of property; titles unsystematic.

**Conclusion.** Latin *custos* is rendered "Warden" (S4, matching King) or "custodian/guardian"; the "servant of Christ's poor" formula (*servus pauperum Christi*) is attested in the Latin. Raymond himself uses *custos*, not *magister*, in the preamble; *magister* is used by Lucius III ("magister vester").

**Uncertainty.** I did not see French *gardiens* in any text I opened; NOT VERIFIED. King's page numbers NOT VERIFIED.

## Q4. Terms

- **Trental.** S2 p. 100-101: chaplains "had to say the trental (or thirty Masses) for every patient who died, although they could farm this out"; p. 118-119: "The Rule had established the trental - the saying of 30 Masses for every dead brother". S3 (Rule ch. 14): "Masses should be sung for his soul for thirty days"; Latin (via summary) "triginta diebus misse pro eius anima cantentur". Conclusion: trental = thirty Masses (thirty days) for a dead brother (and later for dead patients), Rule clause 14.
- **Septaine / quarantaine.** Not found in S3 under those names. S3 ch. 9: fast "seven days, the Wednesday and the Friday on bread and water" and "forty days" for a brother who strikes another; ch. 13 forty days. A search snippet (not opened) glosses septaine as 7 days' loss of habit and quarantaine as 40 days; these terms belong to the **statutes/esgarts**, not necessarily the Rule. Conclusion: for the Rule itself, penance periods of 7 and 40 days; the technical terms *septaine/quarantaine* NOT VERIFIED in the Rule.
- **Master of the church (magister ecclesie).** S2 p. 26: priests "take precedence and are organized into a corps under an official known as the master of the church". S3 ch. 7: "except only those whom the chapter and the master of the church shall send" (Latin via summary: "magistri ecclesie"; verify the case ending). Sarnowsky: not mentioned. Conclusion: head of the priest-brethren / the church of the Hospital.
- **Procurator of the house (procurator domus).** S3 ch. 9: "the procurator of the house shall have heard the complaint"; Latin "procurator domus". S2: titles list puts *procurator domus* in the property-management group; a provincial "master, procurator or prior" has wide jurisdiction (p. 26). Sarnowsky: procurators general at the Curia (later, different office). Conclusion: officer managing the house's property and hearing complaints locally; not the later procurator general.

## Q5. Czech names

- S7 (cs Wikipedia, via summary): "Maltézský řád" / "Řád maltézských rytířů"; originally "Řád johanitů"; also "špitálníci", "hospitalité", "svatojánští rytíři", "rhodští rytíři". Raymond is spelled "Raimund de Pui" there. Wikipedia is not scholarly.
- S8 (Czech scholarly review, PH 31/2, 2024; journal full title NOT VERIFIED): "Suverénní řád Maltézských rytířů, zvaný též johanité"; "České velkopřevorství řádu maltézských rytířů"; Czech arrival "někdy mezi lety 1158 až 1169". Reviewer: Lukáš M. Vytlačil (as printed at head of review).
- Czech scholarly form for Raymond du Puy: **NOT VERIFIED**. Searches turned up no Czech scholarly source on him. Candidates ("Raymond du Puy", "Raimund de Pui" in cs Wikipedia) are unconfirmed; Buben's work (reviewed in S8) is the obvious place to check; not opened.
- Czech for "grand master": cs Wikipedia/S8 context uses "velmistr"/"velkopřevor"; for the 12th-century Raymond the title "mistr" is more accurate (S4/S2 note "grand master" comes later). Not independently verified for Czech.
