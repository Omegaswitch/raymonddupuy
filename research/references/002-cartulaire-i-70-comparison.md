## Headnote of doc. 70 (as printed by Delaville Le Roulx)

- Number and date: **"70. — [1125-1153]"**. Footnote 1: for the dating see his "Statuts de l'ordre de l'Hôpital de S. Jean de Jérusalem", *Bibl. de l'Éc. des Chartes* 48 (1887), 341–56.
- Title: "Raymond du Puy, grand-maître de l'Hôpital, promulgue la règle de l'ordre."
- Sources listed: French text in 14 manuscripts (Dijon, London BM Galba A. 17, Marseille roll 13th c., Montpellier, Paris BN fr. 6049, 1978, 1979, 13531, 1079, 1080, 17255, Vatican 4852 (end 13th c.), Vatican 3136, Turin); Latin text in Aarau (Leuggern commandery no. 7, **copy of 1253**), Florence, Malta (div. I vol. 69; and bullaire, folio now missing), Marseille (17th-c. copy); Provençal, Catalan and Italian texts also listed. Earlier editions: Paoli, *Dell'origine*, app. no. xiv (from Vatican 4852); S. Pauli, *Cod. Dipl.* I, 224 (Latin, from the Malta bullaire).
- Footnote 2: rubrics printed in italics are in red ink in Vatican 4852, "d'après lequel nous publions le texte français". Footnote 3: "Nous publions le texte latin d'après le manuscrit des archives d'Aarau."
- The French text has numbered rubrics 1–19 (as King); the Latin has no numbering or rubrics. King's numbering and rubrics therefore follow the French text; his main text also follows the French, with the Latin differences in his notes — exactly as he says.

So: **Cart. I no. 70 = pp. 62–68, French + Latin**, now verified from the edition itself (previously known only from a secondary web page).

## What this does and does not settle

- **Settled:** the Rule in Cart. I no. 70 occupies pp. 62–68 and is printed in French and Latin; the dating bracket there is [1125-1153]; the Latin opening "custos"; the Old French terms quoted by King are exactly as printed (ysambruns/galembruns, pennes sauvages, fustaines, verges/corroies, toaille, herbergié, chapes, confanon [King: gonfanon], ostel). King's notes on Latin additions (nn. 20-2, 23-1, 23-2, 25-2, 25-3, 26-3?, 27-1) are confirmed in substance.
- **New findings not in King:** §1 Latin "per manum sacerdotis et per librum"; §9 French "ou" vs Latin "ac"; §11 Latin "nisi puram aquam"; §16 Latin names only the Master; §8 the dictionaries contradict "brightly coloured".
- **Still open:** readings marked ★ should be checked on page images; the Provençal/Catalan/Italian texts and the 1300 standard version were not consulted; no critical edition later than 1894 was consulted (Luttrell, "The Hospitallers' Early Statutes", *Revue Mabillon* 75 (2003) — paywalled, not opened).
- **Policy unchanged:** the Czech text remains a translation of King's English; the French/Latin evidence is used only in translator's notes and in the choice of Czech equivalents, never to silently alter King's wording (AGENTS.md principle 1).
