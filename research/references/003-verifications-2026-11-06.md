## V1. Old French dress terms (issue 5)

**Anglo-Norman Dictionary (AND²), entry "isembrun"** — https://www.anglo-norman.net/entry/isembrun
- Headword *isembrun* (1171-74); variants *isenbrun, isebrun*; s. — definition: **"dark-coloured cloth (used for making clothes)"**.
- Citations: (1171-74; MS c.1200) "Desus les altres dras out d'isenbrun … mantel" (l. 5781); (1181-85; MS s.xiv¼) "Nus defendums [les] gallebruns, [E] ensement les isembruns" (l. 1166) — the second is the Anglo-Norman verse *Riwle* (same clause as our §8; the dictionary page does not print the text's name, but the date 1181-85 and the wording match Sinclair's *Hospitallers' Riwle*; identification is ours).

**AND², entry "galebrun"** — https://anglo-norman.net/entry/galebrun
- Headword *[galebrun]* (1181-85); variants *gallebrun, walebroun, walebrun*; s. — definition: **"type of cloth (used for making clothes)"**. Citations include "walebrun de Maence" (cloth of Mainz).

**Other (via search summaries, pages NOT opened):** Wikisource "Lexique de l'ancien français" glosses *galebrun* "sorte d'étoffe commune de couleur foncée"; a Spanish medieval trade vocabulary (um.es, *esembrun*) describes isembrun as a wool cloth in black or brown dyed with iron salts and galls, etymology debated (Germanic *eisen-braun*?). NOT VERIFIED from the pages.

**Latin of the Rule:** "pannos ysambrunos et galambrunos" (Cart. I no. 70; see 002).

**Conclusion:** both words denote kinds of (dark/plain) cloth; *isembrun* is explicitly "dark-coloured" in AND. King's gloss "brightly coloured cloth" is unsupported by the dictionary and is probably a guess. The Czech body text keeps King's wording (fidelity to the source), the translator's note now states the dictionary meaning. A specialist should confirm (Godefroy/Tobler-Lommatzsch not opened).

## V3. Dating: Eugenius III 1153 vs *Christiane fidei religio* 1154 (issue 3)

- fr.wikipedia "Christiane fidei religio" (opened): bull of **Anastasius IV, 1154**, confirming the Order's privileges, placing it under the pope and recognising its right to its own priests; restates a privilege of the same name by Innocent II of **7 February 1137**; cites Riley-Smith 2012 ch. "Brother Priests" and Hiestand, *Papsturkunden für Templer und Johanniter*, NF (1984), pp. 104–135. No Cartulaire number given. Search summaries (not opened) add the date 21 October 1154 — NOT VERIFIED.
- Cart. I no. 690 (opened, OCR): Lucius III (Verona, 4 Nov 1184/85) confirms the Rule which Raymond "de communi consilio et voluntate capituli, salubriter ordinavit, et felicis memorie Eugenius papa, predecessor noster, ut accepimus, confirmavit".
- Delaville Le Roulx dates the Rule itself **[1125-1153]** (Cart. I no. 70 headnote).

**Conclusion:** there is no contradiction to resolve. "1153" (King, Riley-Smith) is the terminus for the papal confirmation of the *Rule* by Eugenius III (d. 8 July 1153); "1154" is the date of the separate privilege *Christiane fidei religio* on *priests*. The only live scholarly question is whether the priest clauses of the Rule pre-date 1154 (Riley-Smith: yes, the Order had priests earlier; Beltjens prefers 1145-53). Historical introduction wording checked; no change needed except that the Cartulaire bracket is now cited from the edition itself. Sinclair's "Cart. no. 217" for the Eugenius confirmation could not be located by number in the OCR (no document "217" string matched); NOT VERIFIED.
