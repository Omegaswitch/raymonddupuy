## 1. Hospital of Jerusalem / Hospitallers
- English: Hospital of Jerusalem; Hospitallers.
- Recommended: **špitál jeruzalémský** (the house); **špitální bratři** (the brothers); **johanité** as the established short name for the Order.
- Alternatives: Špitální řád sv. Jana Jeruzalémského; maltézští rytíři (anachronistic for the 12th c.).
- Evidence: S1 gives "Johanité neboli maltézští rytíři", the original name "Špitální bratři sv. Jana Křtitele Jeruzalémského", the early "špitální bratrstvo", and the official "Řád rytířů špitálu svatého Jana v Jeruzalémě". S18 gives "Suverénní řád maltézských rytířů" with the historical name "Řád Svatého Jana". The exact phrase "špitální řád" is NOT found in S1.
- Caveat: "maltézský" dates from after 1530. For Raymond's era use "špitál" and "špitální bratři". "Špitál" in Czech is a hospice or hospital for pilgrims and the poor, which suits the Latin hospitale.

## 2. Warden / Custos (Raymond's self-title)
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **strážce** or **správce** (špitálu). "Strážce" is closer to custos; "správce" is safer if the English is "warden" in the sense of administrator.
- Evidence: S1 only calls Raymond "mistr Raimund de Pui". No source for "custos" was opened.
- Caveat: Raymond's title in the Rule is not "mistr". If the translation keeps "Warden" distinct from "Master", do not collapse the two in Czech.

## 3. Master
- Recommended: **mistr**. Use **velmistr** only for the later/modern office.
- Evidence: S1 uses "mistr Raimund de Pui" and "velmistr" for the head of the Order. S18 uses "velmistr" throughout and has no bare "mistr".
- Caveat: "velmistr" is the modern standard. For a 12th-c. text "mistr" is the more accurate choice. Check consistency with "Master of the Church" (item 9).

## 4. Chapter
- Recommended: **kapitula**; "generální kapitula" for the general chapter.
- Evidence: S3 uses "provinciální kapitula" and "konventní kapitula". S1 has no occurrence.
- Caveat: In Czech, "kapitula" often means a cathedral chapter (kanovníci). Add "generální", "konventní" or the equivalent when the meeting of the brothers is meant. The place where a chapter is held can be "kapitulní síň".

## 5. Obedience
- Recommended: **podřízený dům** or **závislý dům** (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED).
- Alternative: **obedience** as a transliterated technical term, with a gloss on first use.
- Evidence: none opened. S3 uses "kaplan z poslušnosti" for "chaplain of obedience", which is a different sense.
- Caveat: "Poslušnost" (obedience as a virtue) must stay distinct from "obedience" in the sense of a dependent house.

## 6. Commandery
- Recommended: **komenda**; the head is **komtur**.
- Evidence: S1 "první johanitské komendy" and "česká převorství". S3 has "V jejím čele stál komtur", and "pražský komtur".
- Caveat: Fully developed commanderies are later than 1150, but "komenda" is the standard Czech scholarly term for the house or estate.

## 7. Prior
- Recommended: **převor**.
- Evidence: S2 "V domech rytířských řádů řídí konvent duchovních členů jako podřízený velkopřevora." S1 and S3 use "převor", "velkopřevor" and "podpřevor".
- Caveat: In the early Order the prior was the head of the clerical brothers or of the church, so keep it distinct from "komtur".

## 8. Procurator of the House
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **správce domu** or **hospodář domu**; "prokurátor" is possible but archaizing.
- Evidence: S3 lists "provizor domu" among the modern functions of a convent. Nothing for a medieval "procurator" was opened.

## 9. "Master of the Church"
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **správce kostela**, or **představený kostela** if "Master" is understood as head. S3 lists "inspektor kostela" as a modern function, not an equivalent.
- Caveat: Check how the English edition glosses the office. Do not render it as "mistr kostela", which misleads.

## 10. Serjeant
- Recommended: **služebný bratr** (PROPOSAL); alternative **sloužící bratr**.
- Evidence: S17 lists the modern grade "Sloužící bratr (SBStJ)" (Serving Brother), which is a modern honorary rank. S1 refers to members as "bojových rytířů, kněží a bratří ošetřovatelů". No source was opened for a medieval "serviens" term.
- Caveat: The Czech medieval sources I could open do not settle the term. Choose one rendering and use it consistently. "Sloužící bratr" has modern Order usage, "služebný bratr" is more archaic. The serjeant was a combatant and the rule also uses it for non-knightly brothers, so "zbrojnoš" would be misleading.

## 11. Cleric / lay brother
- Recommended: **klerik** (cleric), **kněz-bratr** or **bratr kněz** (priest-brother), **laický bratr** for lay brother; **konvers** only as a gloss.
- Evidence: S3 uses "klerikové", "kněží", "kaplani" and "kaplan z poslušnosti". Pages for konvers and laický bratr could not be opened (404): NOT VERIFIED.
- Caveat: "Konvers" in Czech monastic usage means a lay brother of a monastery, but the Hospitaller serjeants were not conversi in the Cistercian sense.

## 12. Deacon / subdeacon / acolyte
- Recommended: **jáhen / podjáhen / akolyta**.
- Evidence: S7 "jáhen" (also "diakon" in Orthodox usage). S5 "podjáhen (subdiakon)", with "nižším stupněm svěcení než u jáhna". S6: "Před reformou provedenou Pavlem VI. byl akolytát v římskokatolické církvi nejvyšším z nižších svěcení."
- Caveat: S5 describes the term mainly in an Eastern context. In Latin usage "podjáhen" is standard for the pre-1972 sub-diaconate (major order). Acolyte was a minor order.

## 13. Compline
- Recommended: **kompletář**.
- Evidence: S4 "Kompletář je součástí denní modlitby církve", the last prayer of the day, before sleep. Variants "kompletorium"/"completorium" appear only in interlanguage links.
- Caveat: S4 describes the modern Liturgy of the Hours; the medieval hour is the same.

## 14. Septuagesima
- Recommended: **devítník** (Nedělě devítník, the third Sunday before Lent).
- Evidence: NOT VERIFIED. The pages tried (Devítník, Septuagesima on cs.wikipedia) returned 404. The recommendation is based on standard Czech usage only.
- Caveat: The Roman calendar after 1969 has no Septuagesima.

## 15. Trental
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **třicetidenní mše za zemřelého** (a series of thirty Masses), or **gregoriánské mše** (the specific Gregorian form). "Trentál" as a Czech loanword is NOT VERIFIED.
- Evidence: none; the Wikipedia pages tried returned 404.
- Caveat: Gregorian Masses are a later, specific form of the thirty-Mass series, so use only as a gloss.

## 16. Denier
- Recommended: **denár**.
- Evidence: S10 defines denár as a "stříbrná mince" and says the name was also applied to medieval coins, including the first silver coins minted in Bohemia in the late 10th century. It gives no separate Czech term for pfennig.
- Caveat: For the Rule, the denier is the French penny, so "denár" is acceptable with a footnote.

## 17. Psalter / 150 pater nosters
- Recommended: **žaltář** (the 150 psalms); **sto padesát Otčenášů**.
- Evidence: S9 defines "žaltář" as the book of psalms. It does not mention the number 150 or the link with the Otčenáš. S8: "Otčenáš neboli modlitba Páně", from the Latin Pater noster.
- Caveat: The use of 150 Pater nosters in place of the 150 psalms (for unlettered brothers) is not covered by either page. The wording "místo žaltáře" is mine.

## 18. Last Judgement
- Recommended: **poslední soud** (or **Boží soud**).
- Evidence: S11 "Poslední soud (synonymum: Soudný den)". "Boží soud" appears but is not defined.

## 19. Chastity, obedience, living without property
- Recommended: **čistota, poslušnost a život bez vlastnictví**. Canonical idiom: **čistota, chudoba, poslušnost** (the evangelical counsels).
- Evidence: S12 names the counsels "čistota", "chudoba" and "poslušnost". The phrase "život bez vlastnictví" is not in S12, so I treat it as a literal rendering of "sine proprio".
- Caveat: Use "chudoba" if the translation aims at the established canonical triad; keep "bez vlastnictví" for fidelity to the Rule's wording.

## 20. Fustian
- Recommended: **barchet** (PROPOSAL; "fustián" also used).
- Evidence: S16 defines barchet only as an old length measure for cloth in southern Germany, and does not treat it as a fabric. So that page does not confirm the equivalent. A search summary said barchent/fustian spread to Bohemia, but I did not open a Czech source for the fabric. NOT VERIFIED.

## 21. "dras ysambruns ne galembruns"
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): translate as the Old French says (cloth/robes of the types isambrun/galembrun, kinds of dark or brown cloth: "sukno barvy tmavé"), with the original French in a footnote. No source opened.

## 22. "pennes sauvages" (furs)
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **kožešiny divokých zvířat** / **divoké kožešiny**. No source opened.

## 23. Rods and thongs
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **pruty a řemeny** (for discipline). No source opened.

## 24. Gonfanon
- Recommended: **korouhev**.
- Evidence: S13 defines "korouhev" as a military banner, a symbol of a unit's honour, and also describes church banners hung from a crossbar. It does not mention gonfalon/gonfanon. In vexillology, a korouhev hangs from a horizontal crossbar, and a prapor is fixed to the pole.
- Caveat: Matches the gonfanon's form, but "korouhev" can also mean a processional banner.

## 25. Cassock (chape) / mantle (manteau)
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **plášť s kápí / kabát** for chape, **plášť** for manteau. "Pluviál" and "kasule" are wrong (liturgical). No source opened.

## 26. Wednesday and Friday fast on bread and water
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **ve středu a v pátek se postit o chlebu a vodě**. No source opened.

## 27. Excommunicated
- Recommended: **exkomunikovaný**; the act: **exkomunikace**; plain Czech: **vyobcovaný**.
- Evidence: S14 defines exkomunikace as "vyobcování nebo vyloučení ze svátostného společenství církve". "Klatba" is not in the page: NOT VERIFIED there.

## 28. Holy water, sponge, lantern
- Recommended: **svěcená voda**, **houba**, **lucerna**.
- Evidence: S15 "svěcená voda", and "kropenka" for the stoup. "Houba" and "lucerna" are plain words with no source opened.

## 29. The Body of Our Lord
- Recommended: **Tělo Páně**. NOT VERIFIED by an opened source.

## 30. "Lords the sick"
- Recommended (PROPOSAL, NOT VERIFIED): **naši páni nemocní**.
- Evidence: A search of the phrases "páni nemocní"/"naši páni nemocní" found no confirmation. S18 (Malta Order site) has only "pomoci nemocným a poutníkům", and it has no "páni". Keep the form as a calque of "seigneurs malades" and gloss it in a note.

## 31. Names
- Raymond du Puy: S1 uses **Raimund de Pui** ("mistr Raimund de Pui"). Recommended for a scholarly text: **Raymond du Puy** with the Czech form **Raimund z Le Puy** in the introduction. The Czech Wikipedia page for him returned 404.
- Jerusalem: **Jeruzalém**. S20 and S1 confirm "Jeruzalém" and the adjective "jeruzalémský".
- Portugal: **Portugalsko**. S19.

## 32. 2 Thessalonians 3:12
- ČEP: "Takovým přikazujeme a vybízíme je ve jménu Pána Ježíše Krista, aby žili řádně a živili se vlastní prací." (B1, B2)
- Kralická (BKR): "Protož těm, kteříž takoví jsou, přikazujeme, a napomínáme jich skrze Pána našeho Jezukrista, aby pokojně pracujíce, svůj chléb jedli." (B3; the first part also in B4)
- Český studijní překlad (CSP): "Takovým přikazujeme a vyzýváme je v Pánu Ježíši Kristu, aby s tichostí pracovali a jedli vlastní chléb." (B5)
- B21 (not requested, for comparison): "Takovým přikazujeme a vyzýváme je v Pánu Ježíši Kristu, aby si vydělávali na živobytí řádnou prací." (B2)
- Jeruzalémská bible: NOT VERIFIED. No text found.
- Caveat: The CSP is a scholarly translation prepared by Catholic and other scholars, and is not the same as the Catholic liturgical text. For the "quietness" of the Latin ("cum silentio"), BKR "pokojně" and CSP "s tichostí" are the closest.
